Allow approximately 60–90 minutes each week, with additional reading selected around your patients. Discuss the readings with your supervisor and apply them to one clinical encounter each week.

## How to use this list

The core readings are shared across PGY1 and PGY2. PGY1 residents should focus on structured assessment, initial plans and recognising when to seek help. PGY2 residents should develop more independent plans, explain their reasoning, anticipate complications and coordinate care, with appropriate supervision.

## Week 1 — Assessment, pain and opioids
{: #week-1 }

### Core readings

- [Palliative Performance Scale]({{ '/assets/pdfs/ppsv2.pdf' | relative_url }}) — Victoria Hospice Society.
- [Palliative Performance Scale Instructions for Use]({{ '/assets/pdfs/ppsv2-qa-instructions-definitions-2020.pdf' | relative_url }}) — updated July 2020.
- [BC Centre for Palliative Care — symptom management guidelines](https://www.bc-cpc.ca/symptom-management-guidelines/) — read the **Pain** chapter.
- [Fraser Health — hospice palliative care symptom guidelines](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/employees/clinical-resources/hospice-palliative-care) — read **Principles of opioid management**. This collection includes older editions; check the date on each PDF.

**Apply to a patient:** Present a multidimensional assessment, document PPS, and discuss a pain management plan, including monitoring and follow-up.

**PGY1 focus:** Describe pain mechanisms, review current analgesia and adverse effects, and propose an initial plan.

**PGY2 focus:** Discuss opioid conversion or rotation, organ dysfunction, toxicity and the rationale for adjuvant treatment.

## Week 2 — Communication, consent and common symptoms
{: #week-2 }

### Core readings

- [Ariadne Labs — Serious Illness Conversation Guide](https://www.ariadnelabs.org/resources/downloads/serious-illness-conversation-guide/) — use the guide to prepare for an observed conversation.
- [CPSO — Clinical Care Obligations](https://www.cpso.on.ca/physicians-physician-assistants/professional-resources/professional-obligations/clinical-care) — review the current consent and end-of-life care expectations.
- [BC Centre for Palliative Care — symptom management guidelines](https://www.bc-cpc.ca/symptom-management-guidelines/) — read **Dyspnea**, **Nausea and vomiting**, and **Constipation**, focusing on assessment and management sections.

**Apply to a patient:** Conduct a supervised goals-of-care discussion and document the patient's priorities, decision-maker, agreed plan and unresolved questions.

**PGY1 focus:** Elicit understanding and preferences, respond to emotion and identify the appropriate decision-maker.

**PGY2 focus:** Make a recommendation linked to the patient's goals and address uncertainty or disagreement with supervisor support.

## Week 3 — Delirium, the dying patient and emergencies
{: #week-3 }

### Core readings

- [BC Centre for Palliative Care — symptom management guidelines](https://www.bc-cpc.ca/symptom-management-guidelines/) — read **Delirium** and **Respiratory congestion**.
- [Fraser Health — hospice palliative care symptom guidelines](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/employees/clinical-resources/hospice-palliative-care) — review **Spinal cord compression**, **Hypercalcemia**, **Malignant bowel obstruction**, and **Severe bleeding**. Use these as a starting point for discussion; some chapters are older.
- [Palliative EZ Guide](https://palliativeezguide.ca/) — supplementary practical reference; free registration required.

**Apply to a patient:** Discuss an anticipatory care plan for deterioration, including family communication, symptom assessment, medication routes, nursing instructions and escalation.

**PGY1 focus:** Recognise delirium and possible reversible contributors; identify urgent problems and seek timely help.

**PGY2 focus:** Balance investigation and treatment with goals of care, and explain a plan for a complex symptom or emergency.

## Week 4 — Refractory symptoms, whole-person care and care after death
{: #week-4 }

### Core readings

- [Fraser Health — hospice palliative care symptom guidelines](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/employees/clinical-resources/hospice-palliative-care) — read **Refractory symptoms and palliative sedation therapy** and **Nurturing psychosocial and spiritual well-being**. Review edition dates and discuss local practice with your supervisor.
- [CPSO — Clinical Care Obligations](https://www.cpso.on.ca/physicians-physician-assistants/professional-resources/professional-obligations/clinical-care) — review current end-of-life and MAID obligations.
- [Ontario — Handbook on Medical Certification of Death (PDF hosted on MedicalLinks)]({{ '/assets/pdfs/ontario-medical-certification-of-death-handbook.pdf' | relative_url }}) — focus on cause-of-death statements and when to contact the coroner; confirm current local certification workflow.

**Apply to a patient:** Discuss refractory suffering, family support and bereavement needs with the interdisciplinary team. Practise a cause-of-death statement with your supervisor.

**PGY1 focus:** Identify psychosocial, cultural and spiritual needs, and describe the distinction between symptom relief, palliative sedation and MAID.

**PGY2 focus:** Discuss the assessment and consultation required for refractory symptoms, and lead a coordinated care or discharge plan.

## Further reading — choose two topics
{: #further-reading }

Select two topics prompted by your patients and bring a short clinical question to teaching:

- Wound care, malignant wounds or pressure injury.
- Methadone: [Canadian Virtual Hospice Learning Hub](https://www.virtualhospice.ca/learninghub).
- Prognostic uncertainty: [PPS — UCSF ePrognosis](https://eprognosis.ucsf.edu/pps.php).
- Palliative care in non-cancer illness, including frailty, dementia or organ failure.
- Grief, caregiver distress or spiritual suffering.
- A complex symptom from the [BC Centre for Palliative Care guidelines](https://www.bc-cpc.ca/symptom-management-guidelines/).

**End-of-rotation reflection:** What changed in your approach to symptom assessment, communication and care planning? Identify one learning need to carry forward.
