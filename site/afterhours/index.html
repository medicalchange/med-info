Need care when the clinic is closed?

Emergency, urgent and after-hours options near 840 Coxwell Avenue, Toronto

Emergency

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department for chest pain, trouble breathing, signs of a stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech), severe bleeding, a seizure, fainting, sudden severe pain, or thoughts of harming yourself or someone else.

  • Michael Garron Hospital — Emergency Department
    825 Coxwell Avenue, Toronto · open 24 hours
    Emergency entrance off Mortimer Avenue (J Wing). Children are seen here too.
    For a sprain, strain or small broken bone, you may be able to book a time online instead of waiting.
    Emergency department information and minor-injury booking

Urgent but not an emergency

For problems that should be seen soon but are not life-threatening, such as a fever that will not settle, a painful ear or throat, a urinary infection, or a minor injury.

Patients of the South East Toronto Family Health Team

If your family doctor or nurse practitioner is with the South East Toronto Family Health Team, you can book an after-hours urgent clinic appointment on weekday evenings and on Saturdays. These clinics are for urgent problems only and are booked by phone. There are no walk-ins.

  • Coxwell Medical Centre
    840 Coxwell Avenue, Suite 105 · 416-469-6464, then press 9
  • Carswell Family Health Centre
    1871 Danforth Avenue, 2nd floor · 416-699-7775, then press 9

Current urgent clinic days, hours and locations

Prescription renewals, forms, test results and routine follow-up are not handled in urgent clinics. Book these during regular hours.

Other patients

Call your own family doctor or nurse practitioner’s office. Many practices offer after-hours clinics, and the phone message often explains what to do when the office is closed.

Advice from a nurse, any time

  • Health811
    Call 811 · TTY 1-866-797-0007 · 24 hours a day, free
    Talk or chat online with a registered nurse about symptoms and whether you need to be seen. Health811 can also help you find walk-in clinics and other local services. You do not need an OHIP card. Health811 does not diagnose or renew prescriptions.
    Chat online or find a service

Your pharmacist

Ontario pharmacists can assess and prescribe treatment for many common conditions, such as pink eye, seasonal allergies, cold sores, some skin rashes and uncomplicated bladder infections. They can also extend many ongoing prescriptions until your next appointment.

  • Not every pharmacy offers every service. Call before you go.
  • Bring your OHIP card and a list of your medications.

Mental health crisis

If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

  • 9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline
    Call or text 988 · 24 hours a day
  • Toronto Community Crisis Service
    Call 211 · 24 hours a day · ages 16 and older
    Trained crisis workers can come to you for a mental health crisis that is not a medical emergency.

Poisoning or medication mistake

  • Ontario Poison Centre
    1-844-764-7669 (1-844-POISON-X) · 24 hours a day, free
    Call if someone has swallowed, breathed in or touched something harmful, or taken the wrong medication or dose. If the person is unconscious, having a seizure or has trouble breathing, call 911.
